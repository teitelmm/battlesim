Reasons why the Demand Curve is Downward Sloping

  • : a lower price raises your purchasing power, so you can buy more.
  • : a lower price makes the good cheaper than its substitutes, so you switch to it.
  • : each extra unit is worth less to you, so you'll only buy more at a lower price.

A change in price does not shift the demand curve. It causes a movement along the curve (a change in quantity demanded).
An increase in demand shifts the demand curve to the right. A decrease in demand shifts it to the left.

Normal Goods

As one's income increases, one purchases normal goods.

Inferior Goods

As one's income increases, one purchases inferior goods.

Determinants of Elasticity of Demand

More substitutes → more elastic
Bigger share of your budget → more elastic
Necessities → inelastic; luxuries → elastic
More time → more elastic

Price Ceilings (a legal maximum price, like rent control)

Effective Price Ceilings are equilibrium price.

A price ceiling causes Deadweight Loss, which is a triangle with the sides being the actual quantity sold, the demand curve and the supply curve.

Price Floors (a legal minimum price, like the minimum wage)

Effective Price Floors are equilibrium price.

Marginal Utility divided by Price equals how much satisfaction one gets per dollar from the purchase of that product.

A buyer will purchase the item that gives them the most marginal utility divided by price and will continue buying as much as they can afford. Utility is maximized when MUxPx = MUyPy and the whole budget is spent.
